# Proven Instagram carousel frameworks

A 6-slide carousel earns the swipe on slide 1 and the save/share on slide 6.
Pick ONE framework per carousel and map the 6 slides onto its beats. Don't mix.

## Slide roles (universal)
- **Slide 1 — Hook.** Stops the scroll. A bold claim, a sharp question, a
  pattern interrupt, or a number. No logo dump, no "swipe →" begging. The
  headline does the work.
- **Slide 2 — Standalone alternate hook.** Instagram re-serves carousels to
  people who scrolled past slide 1 — but the second time it leads with *slide
  2*. So slide 2 must work as its own entry point, NOT a continuation of slide
  1: a different angle on the same promise. If slide 2 only makes sense after
  slide 1, that second-chance distribution is wasted.
- **Slides 3–5 — Body.** Deliver on the hook. One idea per slide. Short.
- **Slide 6 — CTA / payoff.** Resolve the tension and tell them exactly what to
  do next. Reinforce the brand. See the CTA menu below — "follow for more" is
  dead; lead with a save trigger or a shop action.

## Framework A — Problem / Agitate / Solution (PAS)
1. Hook: name the painful problem the product solves.
2. Agitate: make the cost of the problem vivid (time, money, embarrassment).
3. Stakes: what it's quietly costing them right now.
4. Solution: introduce the product as the turn.
5. Proof: how it works / a result / a mechanism.
6. CTA: shop now / link in bio.

## Framework B — Listicle ("N ways / N reasons / N mistakes")
1. Hook: "5 reasons [audience] switched to [product]".
2–5. One reason / tip / mistake per slide, each a punchy headline.
6. CTA: round it up + drive the action.

## Framework C — Before → After (transformation)
1. Hook: the "before" state, dramatized.
2. The hidden reason the old way fails.
3. The shift / new approach.
4. The "after" state with the product.
5. Proof point (review, stat, result).
6. CTA.

## Framework D — Myth-busting
1. Hook: "Everyone thinks [common belief]. They're wrong."
2–4. Bust 2–3 myths, one per slide.
5. The truth + how the product reflects it.
6. CTA.

## Framework E — Story / founder
1. Hook: a tension-loaded moment ("We almost shut down in month 3.").
2–4. The arc: struggle → insight → build.
5. What that means for the product the reader holds.
6. CTA.

## Copy rules
- Headlines ≤ 8 words where possible (aim for ≤ 60 characters so it stays big
  and legible on mobile). One idea per slide. Verbs over adjectives.
- Write like a real person who found something useful, not a brand manager.
  Short fragments, specifics, and numbers beat polished ad-speak.
- Match the brand voice you extracted (formal vs playful, emoji or not).
- Use the brand's own product/benefit language pulled from the live site.
- **Banned words** (read as ad-copy, kill them): unlock, transform, discover,
  game-changing, must-have, level up, life-changing, revolutionary, ultimate,
  secret, hack, masterclass. Find a plainer, more specific phrasing instead.
- Never invent claims, prices, stats, or testimonials. If it's not on the site
  or supplied by the user, don't assert it.

## CTA menu (slide 6)
Pick the one that fits the goal — never "follow for more":
- **Shop action** — "Shop the [product] — link in bio." Best for warm,
  bottom-funnel product carousels.
- **Save trigger** — "Save this for your next [moment]." Highest saves; saves
  are the signal the algorithm rewards most.
- **Comment-keyword funnel** — "Comment [WORD] and I'll DM you [thing]." Drives
  comments + opens a DM path. Strongest for reach on cold audiences.
Keep it to ONE clear action. Put it in a short `subline` or the caption, not big
in the art.

## Visual rules

Headlines are NOT generated by the image model — they're composited afterward
with a real font (`compose_text.py`). So image prompts describe a **text-free
background**, and the headline copy + styling lives in each slide's `type` block.

Background prompt rules (go into every image prompt):
- 1080×1350 (Instagram portrait 4:5).
- Wavy / organic color-blocked backgrounds using the EXTRACTED brand palette.
- **ABSOLUTELY NO text, words, letters, or numbers anywhere in the image.**
- Reserve a clean **open negative-space area** (usually the top third) for the
  headline overlay — keep products/objects out of it.
- Product hero where relevant (slide 4/5 and the CTA), clean studio lighting.
- Consistent palette and layout system across all 6 slides so they read as one
  set. Vary composition, not identity.
- Leave safe margins; nothing critical within 80px of any edge.
- No watermarks, no stock-photo feel, no generic AI gloss.

Headline (`type` block) rules:
- One short headline per slide; let it be BIG and bold.
- High contrast against the reserved area: dark type on light slides, cream/
  light type on dark slides.
- Keep CTAs/fine print to a short `subline` (or the IG caption), not the art.
