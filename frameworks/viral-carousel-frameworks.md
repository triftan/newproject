# Viral faceless carousel frameworks

For organic-growth carousels that read as **found, not made** — candid,
iPhone-shot lifestyle content, not branded ads. Use this with the
`viral-carousel` skill. (For polished branded/product carousels use the
`carousel` skill + `carousel-frameworks.md` instead.)

The bet: carousels beat reels right now because the viewer controls the pace —
that illusion of control buys longer attention and more **saves**. And they
automate cleanly without losing quality.

## Five formats that consistently print
Pick ONE per carousel. Each optimizes a different metric — choose by goal:

- **Non-negotiables** — "the [N] non-negotiables of my [X]." Reads as identity
  content the viewer wants to remember. **Highest saves.**
- **Green flags** — "green flags in a [person / place / product]." Viewers send
  it to friends who match. **Highest DM shares.**
- **Hear me out** — opens contrarian, defends the take across the slides.
  Viewers want to agree or argue. **Highest comments.**
- **Ranked list** — 5–7 items in reverse order, strongest reveal on the LAST
  slide. **Highest swipe-through completion.**
- **Before and after** — one transformation told across the slides. **Sustained
  engagement** across the whole sequence.

## Slide architecture (8 slides)
- **Slide 1 — PRIMARY HOOK.** Specific, not generic (generic hooks → generic
  engagement). Under 10 words, stop-the-scroll energy. Use one archetype:
  contrarian statement, listicle promise, or transformation tease. Never "Did
  you know", "You won't believe", or any clickbait opener.
- **Slide 2 — STANDALONE ALTERNATE HOOK.** Instagram re-serves carousels to
  people who scrolled past slide 1 — but the second time it leads with slide 2.
  If slide 2 is just a continuation, that second-chance distribution flops.
  Write it as its own entry point: different angle, same promise.
- **Slides 3–7 — ONE IDEA EACH.** One specific point per slide with a supporting
  visual. Each stands alone but connects into a coherent narrative. Same format
  throughout (numbered list, ranked items, etc.).
- **Slide 8 — SOFT CTA / SAVE TRIGGER.** One clearly stated action. Never
  "follow for more" — that's dead.

## Copywriting rules
This is the difference between a carousel that converts and one that gets buried.
- Write like a real person who just found something useful, not a brand manager.
  Short fragments. Numbers. Plain language. Specific rituals. One idea per slide.
- Keep overlay copy under **60 characters** where possible.
- Match the image's emotional register to the hook: money hook → aspirational
  lifestyle; fitness → movement; skincare → clean close-up texture.
- The product belongs **INSIDE** the content, never bolted on, never a hero
  shot. Show it as a natural step in the world the carousel depicts — e.g. a
  focus supplement appears once as a 10am ritual, not as a product photo.
- **Banned words** (instantly read as an ad): unlock, transform, discover,
  game-changing, must-have, level up, life-changing, revolutionary, ultimate,
  secret, hack, masterclass.

## CTA menu (slide 8)
One action only:
- **Save trigger** — "Save this for your next [moment]." Highest saves.
- **Send trigger** — "Send to a friend who [trait]." Highest DM shares.
- **Comment-keyword funnel** — "Comment [WORD] and I'll DM you [thing]."
  Stacked version (higher-quality comments the algorithm weights more): "Comment
  [WORD] and tell me [your X]."

## Visual rules — "found, not made"
Headlines are NOT generated by the image model — they're composited afterward
(`compose_text.py`). So image prompts describe a **text-free background**, and
the overlay copy + styling lives in each slide's `type` block.

Bake these into EVERY background prompt:
- **Natural light only** — soft, diffused, window-driven. No studio lighting, no
  magazine/editorial polish, no travel-ad gloss.
- **Candid iPhone-style feel** — slightly imperfect, ordinary real-life texture,
  real rooms, real objects.
- **Faceless if a person appears** — back of head, cap, hood, downward gaze,
  side silhouette, hands-only, top-down POV, or cropped from the chest down.
  Never a mirror selfie, never a clear face.
- **Consistent aesthetic across all 8** — same color grade, light, and camera
  feel so the set reads as one shoot. Vary the scene, not the vibe.
- Reserve a clean area for the overlay (centered or bottom-third — same on every
  slide). Keep the subject out of it.
- 1080×1350 (Instagram 4:5) — or 1080×1920 for TikTok. **Same aspect ratio on
  every slide**; Instagram crops later slides to match slide 1, so a mismatch
  causes visible cropping.
- NO text/words/letters/numbers baked in. No watermarks, no fake brand text, no
  extra logos. No uncanny hands, no warped objects, no duplicated limbs.

## Overlay styling (`type` block)
The native look is plain **white bold** type — anything fancier starts to look
"made." Defaults for this skill:
- `font`: Inter-SemiBold (clean neo-grotesque, Helvetica-like), white.
- `align`: center. `valign`: center or bottom — pick one and use it on ALL 8.
- `accent`: null (no rule bar — too "designed" for organic).
- `shadow`: true (soft drop shadow so white type stays legible over photos; use
  `scrim` instead only if a background is especially busy).
- Same size/placement on every slide for consistency.
