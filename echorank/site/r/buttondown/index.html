Executive summary

Buttondown is doing the on-site work: the highest AI-readiness score of the three brands we've published (80/100), llms.txt shipped, five schema.org types, and first-party comparison pages that rank in the retrieval layer for their matchups — a playbook rivals should copy. The problem is the frame. When engines answer 'best newsletter platform', beehiiv and Substack open every list and Buttondown appears late, tagged 'for technical writers who like markdown'. That one-line positioning — accurate years ago — is now the ceiling on every AI answer about the product. No Wikipedia article, a thin review-site base, and near-zero YouTube presence mean engines have little third-party material to contradict the stereotype.

Fix roadmap — highest leverage first

The five moves that most improve how often AI engines say “Buttondown”.

  1. Break the 'for technical writers' one-linerMedium effort

    That phrase is now the ceiling on every generic answer. Publish flagship non-technical case studies (food writers, journalists, artists) and push them into the roundups engines quote, so the retrieval layer has a new sentence to repeat.

  2. Fix the missing homepage H1Low effort

    The probe found no H1 — the one blemish on an otherwise excellent readiness score, and heading structure is what engines use to extract the brand's own claim about itself.

  3. Extend the comparison-page playbook to every rival and win the roundupsMedium effort

    The beehiiv matchup page already wins its query; replicate for Substack/Kit/Ghost, then pitch inclusion higher up the third-party listicles that generic answers are assembled from.

  4. Ride the Substack-exodus narrative harderLow effort

    Migration stories ('moved to Buttondown') are already in the citation layer organically. A migration hub + amplified switcher stories turns a trickle into the default alternative slot.

  5. Build third-party proof: reviews and YouTubeHigh effort

    Two channels at absent/weak where beehiiv saturates. A review-collection push and creator collaborations give engines material that isn't self-authored.
